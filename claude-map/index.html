Three kinds of words, and people mix them up

Every name in that list above is one of three things. Once you know which, the confusion mostly goes away.

The helper

Claude

The actual thinking part. There is only one of these. It's the same Claude whether you're on your phone, in a terminal, or in a spreadsheet.

The rooms

Chat · Cowork · Code

Three kinds of work Claude can be doing. Talking with you. Working on your documents. Working on your code.

The doors

Remote Control · Dispatch · the web · Slack

Ways to reach a room when you're not sitting in front of it. A door is not a new helper. It's a hallway.

Almost every “wait, aren't these the same thing?” moment is two different doors that lead to the same room. They feel identical because the thing at the end is identical. What's different is the hallway.

The rooms

Where the work actually happens

Same helper in all three. What changes is what it's allowed to touch and what it hands you back at the end.

Chat

Where you find it
claude.ai, the phone app, the Chat tab in the desktop app.
What it does
Answers questions, explains things, writes and rewrites, thinks out loud with you.
What it does not do
It isn't digging through the folders on your computer on its own.

Picture itTalking to a very well-read friend across the kitchen table.

Cowork

Where you find it
The Cowork tab in the Claude desktop app.
What it does
You point it at a real folder. It opens the files, works across them, and hands back a finished thing: a slide deck, a spreadsheet, a report, a tidied-up folder.
Who it's for
People doing office and research work who don't want a command line.

Picture itHanding someone your messy folder and getting a finished report back.

Claude Code

Where you find it
The terminal, your code editor, the Code tab in the desktop app, claude.ai/code.
What it does
Reads a whole codebase, edits files, runs commands and tests, saves work to git, opens pull requests.
Who it's for
People who write software.

Picture itA builder in the workshop, with every tool on the wall.

The doors

Four ways in — but only two computers

This is where nearly everyone gets tangled. All four of these let you hand Claude a job while you're away from your desk. The real difference is whose computer does the work.

The two that trip everyone up

“But aren't both of them remote?”

Yes. Both let you work from far away. So being far away is not the difference — and the name “Remote Control” makes that worse. The real difference is one thing: who starts the job.

If it had a clearer name

“Join the job I already started”

That is all Remote Control does. The job exists. You are stepping into it from somewhere else.

If it had a clearer name

“Go start a job for me”

That is all Dispatch does. There is no job yet. You are asking for a new one to begin.

Remote Control

Tuesday — fixing a bug

  1. 9:00

    At your desk. You start Claude Code yourself and say: “fix the bug on the login page.” It gets to work.

  2. 9:15

    You have to leave. You leave the laptop on and walk out.

  3. 9:30

    On the bus. You open Claude on your phone. The same chat is there — same words, same job, still running.

  4. 9:32

    Claude asks: “should I update the tests too?” You tap yes.

  5. 9:40

    You read what it did and type: “also change the button to say Sign in.”

You started the job. Your phone was just a second screen for something that was already going.

Dispatch

Wednesday — at the dentist

  1. 9:00

    You never opened your laptop today. It is sitting at home, awake, with the Claude app open. Nothing is running.

  2. 9:05

    You text Claude: “pull last month's sales numbers into a spreadsheet.”

  3. 9:06

    Dispatch reads it, decides this is document work, and starts the job by itself.

  4. 9:25

    Your phone buzzes: “Done — here is the spreadsheet.”

  5. 9:26

    You never opened anything. You never picked a tool. You just asked.

There was no job until you asked for one. Claude started it, and Claude decided how to do it.

The case that looks identical

When Dispatch opens Claude Code by itself

This is the moment that makes people give up. Watch what happens — and then watch how you got there.

Dispatch

Thursday — walking the dog

  1. 2:00

    You text Claude: “the sign-up button on the site is broken, can you look?”

  2. 2:01

    Dispatch reads it. This one is code work, not document work — so it opens a Claude Code session on your desktop on its own.

  3. 2:35

    Your phone buzzes: “Fixed it. I opened a pull request.”

Now the end result looks exactly like Remote Control: Claude Code, running on your machine, you on your phone. But you never opened Claude Code, and you never asked for it. Dispatch chose it.

Only Remote Control can…

  • Show the same chat on your laptop and your phone at once. Type on either one — it is one conversation.
  • Let you jump in halfway and change direction: “stop, do it the other way.”
  • Let you watch every single step as it happens.
  • Use the exact project you already had open, with all its settings.

Only Dispatch can…

  • Start a job when nothing is running. You do not have to set anything up first.
  • Work out for itself whether your request is document work or code work.
  • Be used by someone who has never opened a terminal.
  • Take a one-line request and go quiet until it is finished.

Remote Control is a steering wheel.

The car is already moving. You are driving it — you are just sitting further away than usual.

Dispatch is a taxi.

Nothing is moving yet. You say where you want to go, and somebody else does the driving.

The three that get mixed up most

If you only remember one thing from this page, make it this table.

Question Remote Control Dispatch Code on the web
Who starts the job? You do, at your desk, before you leave. Claude does, after you ask. You do, from the browser.
What you do Start a session at your desk, then pick it up on your phone. Message a task from your phone. Nothing started yet. Open a browser and describe the job.
Which computer works Yours. Yours. A rented one in the cloud.
Must your computer be on? Yes — and Claude Code already running on it. Yes — the desktop app has to be open. No — close the laptop and walk away.
Which room does it reach? Claude Code, always. Cowork first — it opens Claude Code only if the job needs it. Claude Code, always.
Best when… You're mid-job and want to keep steering it from the couch. You thought of something while out and just want it handled. You want to kick off work with zero setup, or several jobs at once.

Neither a room nor a door

Things Claude picks up while it works

A few more names you'll run into. These aren't places and they aren't hallways — they're equipment, and they work in every room.

Skills

A recipe card Claude reads before starting. “Here's how we make slide decks around here.” It picks up the right one on its own.

Connectors

A plug that lets Claude reach something outside itself — Gmail, Notion, GitHub, your calendar — instead of only files.

Artifacts

A finished page Claude builds and gives you a link to. You're reading one right now.

Subagents

Helpers Claude hires for a piece of a big job, so several parts get done at once. They report back and disappear.

Scheduled tasks

An alarm clock. It starts one of the doors on a timer — every morning, every Monday — without you asking again.

The API

The plain wire into Claude, for programmers building their own apps on top of it. No rooms, no doors — just the helper.
